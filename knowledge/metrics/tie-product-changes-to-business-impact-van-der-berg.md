# How to Tie Product Changes to Business Impact

**Author:** Else van der Berg
**Source:** [Else's Productpourri](https://elsevanderberg.substack.com/p/how-to-tie-product-changes-to-business)
**Date:** November 15, 2023

---

## Core Thesis

Shipping features customers want and that drive measurable business results requires structured thinking. Building features is easy. The difficulty lies in shipping the *right* features and driving business impact.

---

## Key Frameworks

### Hierarchy of Metrics (Levels 1-3)

A top-down approach establishing the connection between company goals and product metrics:

- **Level 1:** Company goal (e.g., revenue growth)
- **Level 2:** Business metrics broken down by revenue stream
- **Level 3:** Product metrics (leading indicators that drive business outcomes)

### Business vs Product Metrics Distinction

References Teresa Torres' framework:

- **Business metrics:** Organization's overarching goals; lagging indicators
- **Product metrics:** Measure customer value delivery; leading indicators more useful for decision-making

### Opportunity Solution Tree Integration

The framework merges the Hierarchy of Metrics with Teresa Torres' Opportunity Solution Tree methodology:

```
Company Goals → Business Impact Metrics → Customer Opportunities → Solution Ideas
```

---

## Five-Step Implementation Process

### Step 1: Identifying & Aligning Outcomes

- Leadership defines overarching company goal with boundary metrics
- Example boundary: "maintain NPS above 50" while growing revenue
- Distribute goals across products/revenue streams

### Step 2: Levels 1-2-3 Metrics

- Teams identify 3-5 metrics driving each level above (MECE principle)
- Back metrics with proof points from data
- Note: "increasing customer retention rates by 5 percent has the potential to increase profits by 25-29 percent" (Harvard Business Review)

### Step 3: Customer Opportunities

- Map business metrics to customer pain points
- Use customer insights, surveys, analytics
- Apply iterative "why" questioning to drill deeper
- Example: Why aren't users reaching their "aha moment"?

### Step 4: Potential Solutions

- Describe solutions in 1-3 lightweight sentences
- Use impact/risk matrices to prioritize
- Define minimum viable tests (balancing rigor with efficiency)
- Avoid over-engineering validation or under-testing

### Step 5: Ex-Post Analysis

Two critical questions:
1. Are users adopting and regularly using the feature?
2. Has the expected business metric moved?

Investigate *why* through analytics, customer interviews, and usability testing.

---

## Practical Guidance

### Avoiding Common Pitfalls

- Don't confuse business outcomes with product outcomes when asking discovery questions
- Specify product metrics (e.g., "free-to-paid upgrade rate") rather than business objectives
- Ensure features receive launch communications or expected impact won't materialize

### Cross-Functional Approach

Co-create solution matrices with diverse teams (product, engineering, leadership) to improve decision quality.

### Timeline Flexibility

The timeframe for measuring impact depends on the feature and business model, ranging from weeks to months.

---

## Key Takeaway

Building products with business acumen prevents resource waste, product bloat, and weak market positioning. The framework creates alignment by linking every feature decision transparently to organizational goals.
