# Product Vision

Build a clear, inspiring product vision through structured co-creation. A vision defines the world you're creating, not the product you're building.

## Soft Prerequisites

None. Vision is the starting point. If strategy, metrics, or roadmap work feels directionless, come here first.

## Co-Creation Flow

```
Purpose → Future State → Impact → Vision Statement
```

Work through each element one at a time. For each: explain the concept, ask one question, reflect back what you heard, confirm before moving on.

### Element 1: Purpose

**Explain:** Purpose is why the product or company exists beyond making money. It's the fundamental reason your team comes to work. Not "to build software" but "to make small business owners feel confident about their finances."

**Ask:** "Why does this product exist? If it disappeared tomorrow, what would the world lose?"

**Reflect/Confirm:** Restate the purpose in one sentence. Confirm it resonates before continuing.

### Element 2: Future State

**Explain:** The future state is a vivid picture of the world you're creating, 3-5 years from now. Not what the product will do, but what changes for your users and their world. Think of it as "once upon a time, things were hard because X. In the future we're building, Y is true instead."

**Ask:** "Imagine it's 3-5 years from now and your product has succeeded beyond expectations. Describe what's different for your users. What can they do that they couldn't before?"

**Reflect/Confirm:** Play back the future state. Check: is it specific enough to guide decisions? Could any competitor claim the same future? If yes, sharpen it.

### Element 3: Impact

**Explain:** Impact is the measurable change you want to make for a specific audience. It bridges the inspirational future state with something concrete. Not a product metric (DAU, revenue) but a human outcome (time saved, opportunities created, problems eliminated).

**Ask:** "Who specifically benefits from this vision, and what measurable change do you want to create for them?"

**Reflect/Confirm:** Confirm the audience and the change. Check: is the audience specific enough? Is the impact something you can eventually point to and say "we did that"?

### Assembly

Synthesize all three elements into a 1-3 paragraph vision statement. Then distill into a single sentence (the elevator version). Present both and confirm.

## Output Template

```markdown
# Product Vision: [Product Name]

## Purpose
[Why we exist, one paragraph]

## Future State
[The world we're creating, 3-5 years from now]

## Impact
[The measurable change, for a specific audience]

## Vision Statement
[1-3 paragraph synthesis]

**In one sentence:** [Elevator version]
```

## Verification Criteria

After drafting, run these checks. For failures: fix silently if you have the context, ask if you need input. Max 3 attempts per check.

### Structural (4 checks)
- [ ] Purpose section present
- [ ] Future State section present
- [ ] Impact section present
- [ ] Vision Statement present (both full and one-sentence versions)

### Quality (6 checks)
- [ ] No tactical or feature language (no "we will build X")
- [ ] Time horizon is 3+ years (not next quarter)
- [ ] Describes customer/user impact, not company goals (not "we will be the market leader")
- [ ] Specific enough to guide decisions (passes "could any company say this?" test: if yes, too vague)
- [ ] Inspiring but honest (no hyperbole, no buzzwords)
- [ ] Audience is named, not generic ("small business owners" not "our users")

## Common Pitfalls

- **Mission vs vision confusion.** Mission is what you do today. Vision is the future you're creating. "We help teams communicate" is a mission. "A world where distance never prevents collaboration" is a vision.
- **Vague enough for any company.** "To empower people with technology" could be said by any company. Sharpen until it couldn't.
- **Jumping to product features.** "Our vision is to build AI-powered analytics" is a product description, not a vision. What changes in the world because of those analytics?
- **Too short a time horizon.** If your vision could be accomplished in 12 months, it's a goal, not a vision.
- **Internally focused.** "To be the #1 platform" is a company ambition. A vision describes what changes for the people you serve.
